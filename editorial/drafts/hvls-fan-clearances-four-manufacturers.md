# HVLS Fan Clearances: What Four Manufacturers Want Around the Blades

Every HVLS layout comes down to a handful of distances: blade to floor, blade to deck, blade to whatever hangs beside it, and fan to fan. CaptiveAire, Big Ass Fans, MacroAir and Hunter each set those numbers differently, and the tightest one decides where your mounting point goes [1][2][3][4].

Here's what each one lists, so you can lay out the job before the lift shows up. The figures change with fan size and mount, so work from the manual for the exact model you're hanging.

## Floor and reach

- Big Ass Fans, MacroAir and Hunter: bottom of the blades at least 10 ft above the floor [2][3][4].
- CaptiveAire: 144 in, or 12 ft, from the floor to the fan [1].

Hunter calls 20 to 30 ft the preferred height for its 20 to 24 ft fans [4].

Mezzanines, catwalks and walkways count too. MacroAir keeps the blade tips 3 ft from anywhere a person can reach out. Hunter makes it 5 ft [3][4].

## Deck

This is where they split furthest.

- CaptiveAire: 60 in from the top of the blade to the roof deck [1].
- Big Ass Fans Powerfoil 8, measured from the top of the winglets: 4 ft for an 8 to 10 ft fan, 5 ft for 12 to 14 ft, 6 ft for 16 to 18 ft and 7 ft for 20 to 24 ft. The Powerfoil 8 Plus needs a foot more at most sizes, up to 8 ft on a 22 or 24 ft fan [2].
- Hunter: about a quarter of the fan diameter from the ceiling, or roughly 6 ft on a 24 ft fan [4].
- MacroAir sets the drop from a table by fan diameter and roof slope [3].

Put it on a real deck. Under a 30 ft deck, the winglets on a 24 ft Powerfoil 8 Plus can't sit higher than 22 ft. A CaptiveAire fan's blades can go up to 25 ft. Your working window is the space between that line and the floor minimum.

## Beside and above the blades

- Big Ass Fans: every part of the fan 2 ft from any obstruction, measured from the winglet edge [2].
- Hunter: 2 ft of blade clearance from any obstruction [4].
- CaptiveAire: 24 in from the blades to anything above or below, including the bottom of the joists, and 18 in from the side of the fan to any obstruction [1].
- MacroAir: at least 1.5 times the fan diameter from a wall or similar obstruction [3].

The MacroAir wall rule moves fans. On a 24 ft fan it works out to 36 ft, so a fan you'd tuck near an end wall on another brand's layout may need to come out into the bay.

## Lights

Hunter wants 2 ft between the blades and any light fixture [4]. Hunter and MacroAir both say to stay out from directly under lights and skylights, because the spinning blades chop the light into a strobe [3][4]. Big Ass Fans wants its 2 ft clearance zone free of lights, cables and sprinklers [2]. CaptiveAire says more distance between the fixture and the blades, up and across, cuts the strobe [1].

## HVAC and doors

Air blowing into the blades fights the fan.

- CaptiveAire and Big Ass Fans: with the fan level with or above a diffuser, keep 1 fan diameter away. With the fan below it, 2 diameters [1][2].
- MacroAir: where there are diffusers, 1.5 times the fan diameter from any source of airflow [3].
- Hunter: no fan directly under a vertical discharge such as an AC unit or evaporative cooler. That equipment has to sit outside the swept area and at least 2 diameters off, and the fan can't sit in the path of a horizontal discharge at any distance [4].

For a 24 ft fan hung below a diffuser, that's 48 ft by the CaptiveAire and Big Ass Fans rule.

Big Ass Fans and MacroAir also keep fans away from high winds off HVAC equipment and large garage doors [2][3]. Hunter says not to install or run the fan anywhere it's exposed to wind [4]. On a dock wall, that means the bays nearest the doors are a poor spot for a fan.

## Fan to fan

Big Ass Fans spaces its fans at least 2.5 diameters apart, center to center [2]. Two 24 ft fans need 60 ft between hubs, and two 12 ft fans need 30 ft.

## Sprinklers and power

All four put the fan at least 3 ft below the sprinklers. CaptiveAire, Big Ass Fans and Hunter measure to the deflector and center the fan between four heads. MacroAir centers it in the sprinkler quadrant [1][2][3][4]. CaptiveAire caps fans at 24 ft diameter in sprinklered buildings, including ESFR [1]. The fire alarm shutdown wiring is its own job, covered in our sprinkler interlock guide.

Hunter brings fan power to a point 5 ft outside the swept area and mounts the drive panel 5 ft outside it as well [4]. That keeps the drive where a tech can reach it without working under the blades.
