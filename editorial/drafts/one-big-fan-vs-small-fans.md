# Why One Big Fan Beats a Wall of Small Ones

Big Fan Guys crews walk into a lot of buildings where the cooling plan is a dozen loud drum fans bolted to columns. They're cheap to buy. They're also loud, they throw air in a tight cone, and every one of them is another motor running flat out.

A high-volume, low-speed fan works the other way. It turns slowly and moves a huge column of air down to the floor and out across it. Manufacturers rate a single 24-foot HVLS fan to cover somewhere around 14,000 to 20,000 square feet, depending on the model [1][2]. Check the rating for the fan you're quoting, but that's the scale you're working with.

## What changes on the floor

- Comfort evens out. Instead of a cool spot in front of each drum fan and dead air between them, the whole space gets mixed.
- The building gets quieter. A big fan turning slow doesn't drown out the crew, so people can hear each other and the forklift backup alarms.
- Columns and walls open up. One fan on the structure replaces the cords, stands and brackets of a row of small ones.
- Fewer motors to run and maintain. One drive and one motor instead of a dozen.

## Do the math before you buy

Price one HVLS fan, installed, against what the drum fans cost to run, replace and work around. In most warehouses and shops Big Fan Guys works in, that comparison favors the big fan. Your building's layout, ceiling height and obstructions decide how many you need, so get a layout done before you order.
