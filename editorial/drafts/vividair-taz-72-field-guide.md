# Inside the 72-Inch TAZ: A Field Guide to VividAir's Aisle Fan

The 72-inch TAZ occupies a different corner of the air-movement market from a large overhead HVLS fan. VividAir markets the directional unit for aisles, mezzanines and spaces constrained by overhead obstructions, with separate technical documentation for the 72-inch and 36-inch versions [1][2]. Its value on a project depends on where the air needs to go, not simply how much nominal airflow appears in a catalog.

## Identifying the 72-inch equipment

The publisher identifies the two retained installation photographs as a 72-inch TAZ. They show a suspended cylindrical housing, protective grille and louvered discharge arrangement. Nameplate confirmation and final captions remain pending; the photographs establish visible installation details, not model-specific performance.

## What linear coverage does—and does not—describe

VividAir describes TAZ as an in-aisle air-movement product and advertises up to **135 feet of linear cooling coverage** [1]. That is a manufacturer claim, not an independent measurement by HVLS 360°. The published family also includes two size options and claims maximum airflow of up to **75,200 CFM** [1].

The test basis, operating configuration and required air speed at occupied locations should be specified before the published coverage figure is used in a layout. Linear coverage is not equivalent to the circular coverage area of an overhead HVLS fan, and maximum airflow is not a complete measure of occupant comfort.

The manufacturer says the fan can be installed flat or angled and describes one-direction or multidirectional airflow options [1]. Those choices make placement and orientation especially important around racking, mezzanines and other obstructions.

## Two sizes, two technical guides

The current VividAir TAZ product page labels its January 2023 download as the **72-inch Installation and Technical Operations Guide**, alongside a separate 36-inch guide [1][2]. Inside that linked document, however, the headings identify a **7ft TAZ Fan**, while the power-requirements section lists **GFY-TAZ-72-480** and **GFY-TAZ-72-240** [2, printed p. 13]. That inconsistency needs manufacturer clarification against the supplied unit. The download label alone does not establish the unit's dimensions or the applicability of every drawing.

The guide's installation check-in procedure calls for the contractor and customer to review the fan layout, controller locations and supply panels before unloading, and to review the mounting method for each location [2]. Include those decisions in the site survey, before equipment and labor are mobilized.

The existence of a 2023 guide does not establish that every later production unit has the same controls or hardware revision. Match the actual fan nameplate and supplied documents before ordering replacements or following an older wiring diagram.

## Geometry, mounting and service access

A directional aisle fan may have to fit near shelving, structural members or a mezzanine where a conventional large-diameter fan cannot operate. The pre-installation review should therefore address the intended airflow direction, physical clearances, the mounting structure and a safe work position for assembly and service.

Verify the exact manufacturer mounting hardware, load path and fasteners for the chosen configuration. Do not assume that a mounting approach used on a different fan family transfers to the TAZ.

A service technician should also confirm whether the protective screens and discharge louvers remain accessible for inspection after the equipment is installed. Those features are visible in the publisher's photographs and are relevant to maintenance access, but the photographs alone do not establish the required inspection procedure.

## Record the actual controls before troubleshooting

Before troubleshooting a TAZ, capture the exact model, supply voltage, motor and drive information, keypad or controller model, and any interface to the building's control or fire-alarm system. Compare that information against the applicable operations guide rather than relying on a generic Go Fan Yourself or VividAir wiring diagram.

This matters especially at mixed-generation facilities. Legacy documentation may refer to a different controller or electrical arrangement than a current sales sheet. A familiar product name is not enough to establish interchangeability.

## Questions that determine the specification

- Is the objective aisle coverage, localized worker comfort or general room mixing?
- Which direction should the air discharge, and what obstructions affect that path?
- Is the required product the 36-inch or 72-inch configuration?
- Can the chosen mounting method be installed and maintained safely in the available space?
- What controller, power supply and interlock documentation applies to this exact unit?
- Which published performance figures can be compared under equivalent test conditions?

## Establish the discharge path

For a TAZ layout, identify where the air must reach and what blocks its path. Then check the selected size, orientation and mounting arrangement against the applicable guide. Retain the model-specific controls documentation with the installation record, and resolve the linked guide’s size-label inconsistency before treating it as the final installation reference.
